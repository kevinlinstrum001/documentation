central idea

Core thesis

The visible chatbot is not the whole story. The real story is that a new industrial system is being built to manufacture reasoning at scale.

This page is not meant to be a headline feed. It is a lesson map for following the story as it develops. The key question is not only which model is best. The deeper question is:

Can humans learn to observe, understand, and govern artificial reasoning systems before connecting them too deeply to money, labor, infrastructure, persuasion, and power?

Story map

chapter 1

The creature

Large language models and multimodal models are the visible intelligence-like systems: chatbots, coding agents, image/video models, workplace assistants, and future autonomous agents.

chapter 2

The body

The “cloud” is physical: GPUs, CPUs, high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging, power, cooling, data centers, wafer fabs, lithography machines, cables, and supply chains.

chapter 3

The microscope

Interpretability tries to look inside these models: neurons, circuits, features, activation pathways, causal interventions, and AI systems explaining other AI systems.

chapter 4

The law

Safety and governance ask what should be allowed, slowed down, inspected, tested, restricted, or publicly accountable before these systems shape society.

The hardware body: where the “mind” becomes physical

The AI system depends on a narrow and highly specialized production chain. A simplified chain:

  1. NVIDIA designs AI accelerators and full rack-scale systems for “AI factories.”
  2. TSMC manufactures many leading-edge chips and provides advanced packaging such as CoWoS for AI and supercomputing.
  3. ASML makes EUV and High-NA EUV lithography systems needed to print the tiniest chip features.
  4. Memory suppliers such as SK hynix, Samsung, and Micron make HBM, a major bottleneck for AI accelerators.
  5. Cloud and data-center builders assemble the environment: buildings, cooling, networking, power distribution, and operational software.

The microscope: AI looking inside AI

This is the thread closest to the question: Can we study artificial reasoning without simply forcing it to behave? Interpretability asks what is happening inside the network rather than merely judging the output.

key narrative

OpenAI’s neuron-explainer experiment

OpenAI used GPT-4 to write explanations for neurons in GPT-2 and score those explanations. This is an early example of one AI system serving as a tool for examining another.

This is not yet “understanding” by itself. The important next step is whether those explanations survive intervention and prediction tests.

Links: OpenAI article · Neuron-explainer paper

key narrative

Anthropic’s circuit tracing

Anthropic describes interpretability as building a kind of microscope for model internals: tracing features, circuits, and pathways behind model behavior. This is closer to observing the system than asking for a self-report.

Links: Tracing the thoughts of a large language model · Anthropic Interpretability Team · Transformer Circuits

Working idea: use AI as a microscope, not an oracle. The model may generate hypotheses about another model, but humans still need intervention, replication, and falsification.

Safety and governance: who builds the law?

If model labs are building the creature and chip companies are building the body, governance tries to define the limits: what must be tested, paused, inspected, disclosed, restricted, or publicly accountable.

Watch for the gap between voluntary promises and enforceable rules. The key question is not whether labs say they care about safety, but what external tests, audits, legal obligations, and shutdown mechanisms exist.

Open questions for this page

  2. Can “fragment-field prompting” reveal model tendencies that ordinary instruction prompts hide?
  3. Can AI-generated explanations of other AI systems be validated experimentally?
  4. What would count as a stable substructure in artificial reasoning?
  5. Which bottleneck matters more in the near term: chips, memory, power, data, talent, regulation, or interpretability?
  6. What should society refuse to connect to AI until understanding improves?
