Housekeeping state at time of entry to Meadows:

* Paper existed, but there was no functioning record system.
* Supplies were ordered, but there was no inventory system.
* Requests could be texted, but there was no work-request system.
* New employees could shadow someone, but there was no training program.
* SDS documents existed, but there was no usable chemical-knowledge system.
* Employees had assignments, but positions and work were not formally defined.
* Work happened every day, but no day could be reconstructed afterward.
* Standards may theoretically exist somewhere, but inaccessible standards cannot guide operations.

The new page can become the written orientation meeting that never occurred.

## Observed starting state

* No regular housekeeping meetings.
* No documented planning process.
* No visible model of housekeeping operations.
* No written description of how the department functions.
* No accessible SOP collection.
* No accessible definitions of jobs or positions.
* No written standards employees knew how to locate.
* No forward-looking work calendar.
* No schedule showing what work should be done and when.
* No formal request channel for units needing housekeeping assistance.
* Requests generally depended on texting the supervisor.
* No request acknowledgement, assignment, status, or completion record.
* Daily sheets were completed but accumulated in a storeroom.
* Daily sheets were apparently not reviewed or used for planning.
* No searchable operational history.
* No ability to reconstruct a particular day from records.
* No inventory accounting at Meadows.
* A requisition sheet existed for ordering supplies, but did not represent on-hand inventory.
* No records of receiving, moving, issuing, consuming, or correcting inventory.
* No building map identifying storage locations.
* No stable model of units, rooms, closets, carts, dispensers, and other physical objects.
* The SDS system met a basic requirement but did not function as practical employee guidance.
* No documented procedures for conditions such as MRSA.
* No structured onboarding program.
* New employees learned primarily by following an experienced employee.
* No visible training objectives, competency checks, or training records.
* No clear written route for obtaining answers about leave, insurance, union status, evaluations, or other employment questions.
* Unresolved concerns existed around evaluation paperwork and whether documented evaluations matched what employees actually experienced.

## Questions we need to collect

* Who can assign or reassign work?

This should become our **Meadows Housekeeping Operational Gap Inventory**. For the story, we will compress it. For the underlying historical record, we should preserve it in full.

The strongest opening line is probably:

> The work was happening every day. What was missing was a way to define it, plan it, request it, measure it, explain it, or remember it.
